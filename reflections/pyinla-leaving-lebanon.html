Submitted One Day After Leaving Lebanon

A reflection on PyINLA, three years of side-project evenings, and uncertainty

I submitted this manuscript one day after leaving Lebanon.

Before knowing if there would be a way out. A safe road. A flight.

While the gunfire echoed and the uncertainty was total, I wanted to mark the moment. So that one day, looking back, I would remember what it took.

The paper is about PyINLA, a personal side project I've been building over the past three years, entirely on my own time. Weekends. Late nights. Out of genuine curiosity.

My PhD was in statistics and computational statistics. My postdoc took me into high-performance computing and low-level model implementation. PyINLA was my way of bridging the two.

What does it do?

  • Predict elections
  • Detect disease outbreaks
  • Model climate risk
  • Calculate your team's xG
  • Handle millions of data points, with proper uncertainty quantification

No MCMC. No convergence headaches. Just answers, fast.

Maybe that's what I needed that day too. Just answers.

pyinla.org is on its way. More likelihoods, more examples, more applications. This is just the beginning, and I'm genuinely enjoying building it.

The best part is watching it grow into something useful for people I will never meet, and maybe will.

Lebanon teaches me to live with uncertainty.
PyINLA taught me to quantify it.
